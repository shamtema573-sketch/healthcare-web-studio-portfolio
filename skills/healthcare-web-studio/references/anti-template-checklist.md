# Anti-template review

Use this checklist after the first rendered version and again before publication.

## Composition

- Can the first viewport be recognized as this specific clinic or service if the logo and navigation are hidden?
- Is there one clear visual anchor, with the headline, supporting copy, and action supporting it?
- Does the page use one distinctive composition rather than a default hero plus a repeated grid of cards?
- Does the scroll rhythm vary in scale, alignment, density, image use, or background without looking like unrelated templates?
- Can any pill, badge, metric, icon, shadow, gradient, border, or card be removed without losing meaning? Remove it if so.

## Copy

- Does each section give the visitor a useful fact or action?
- Can the reader tell what is fictional, what is implemented, and what is only proposed?
- Does any line promise an outcome, expertise, certification, rating, or speed that the brief does not prove?
- Replace broad slogans such as “care reimagined”, “a new standard”, “your journey starts here”, “the future of wellness”, and “where science meets compassion” with a specific service, process, or next step.
- Remove default brand openings such as “we believe in…”, “more than just…”, “where X meets Y”, and interchangeable “personalised, seamless, exceptional” strings.
- Watch for a repeated machine-like cadence: every section starts with a slogan, every feature has three benefits, every paragraph ends in a promise, or every sentence has the same length. Vary or cut it.
- Remove “best”, “leading”, “world-class”, “cutting-edge”, and “seamless” unless the user supplied evidence and approved the claim.
- Do not replace a cliché with a new vague metaphor. Name the service, what the visitor can compare, or what happens after the click.
- Read each headline and CTA without the design context. If it is still vague, rewrite it.

## Evidence and trust

- Does every clinician, review, price, location, schedule, and credential have a source or a visible demo label?
- Are sample images clearly illustrative and never presented as named staff or patients?
- Does each trust signal help visitors assess the real offer instead of decorating the page?
- Do forms explain what happens after submit? If nothing is transmitted, does the confirmation say that?

## Interaction and inclusion

- Can every button, filter, menu, dialog, accordion, language control, and form be used with a keyboard?
- Are focus, selected, invalid, disabled, empty, and completed states visible and understandable?
- Are contrast, touch targets, small-screen overflow, zoom, and reduced-motion behavior checked?
- Does every section remain useful with motion disabled and images not loaded?

## Final score

Score each area from 0 to 2: specificity, composition, copy, trust, interaction, accessibility, responsive behavior, asset provenance, and performance.

- 0 means a real problem remains; fix it before handoff.
- 1 means acceptable but worth improving; note the limitation.
- 2 means the result is specific, verified, and works as intended.

Never average away a zero in medical trust, data handling, or core interaction behavior.
